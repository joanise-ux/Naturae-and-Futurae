The orchard in Walim — over a century beneath the Owl Mountains

"The old trees survived two wars and half a century of neglect. We carry their genes into the future — vial by vial."

The Owl Mountains region, the estates of the von Zedlitz and von Seher-Thoss families, the palace in Jedlinka. From 18th-century orchards, through wars and the communist era, all the way to our in vitro laboratory in Wałbrzych.

// roots

The land of the von Zedlitz and von Seher-Thoss families

At the foot of the Owl Mountains, among the estates of the aristocratic von Zedlitz and von Seher-Thoss families, orchards and gardens were planted from the 18th century onward. The palace in Jedlinka was the heart of this land — and around it grew apple, pear and plum varieties chosen by generations of gardeners.

Walim, lying along the same route, inherited that horticultural instinct. The orchard that arose here at the turn of the century combined Silesian and Saxon varieties — hardy trees suited to the mountain climate and the short summer.

// timeline

From orchard to vial

~1900

The orchard in Walim is founded

The first trees — Silesian and Saxon varieties — planted on the slopes beneath the Owl Mountains. Antonówka, Glogierówka, Kosztela: apple trees bred to endure mountain winters.

1939–45

Wars — and the trees grew on

Borders shifted, estates changed hands, but the orchard endured. The old apple trees blossomed every spring, regardless of who ruled the valley.

1945–89

The communist era — neglected, but alive

Half a century without care. Many varieties vanished from maps and catalogues — but here, on the slope, the pre-war trees still bore fruit, a silent archive of old pomology.

2020+

Nature & Future — new life for old varieties

We take a tiny fragment of tissue, clear it of viruses and establish an in vitro culture. The old orchard lives on — in hundreds of plants, free of disease, ready for a new century.
