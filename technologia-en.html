§ 01

What is in vitro?

plant micropropagation
PL. 014 · in vitro · tissue culture

It is a method of propagating plants in sterile laboratory conditions — from a microscopic fragment of tissue (the explant) a new, independent plant grows, genetically identical to the mother.

Instead of seeds, cuttings or grafts — glass, medium and growth regulators. Thousands of plants from a single leaf, on a scale no traditional nursery could handle. No pathogens, no viruses, no loss of varietal traits.

§ 02

Six stages of the process

from leaf to seedling · 6–14 weeks
stage I · ~1 day
Taking
the explant

From the mother plant we cut a tissue fragment — a shoot tip, a piece of leaf, a fragment of root. This is the explant.

stage II · ~30 min
Surface
sterilisation

A bath in a solution of hypochlorite, ethanol and detergent. We eliminate bacteria and fungi on the surface — anything that could destroy the culture.

stage III · 2–4 wks
Initiation
on medium

The explant is placed on an agar medium (Murashige & Skoog) with sugar, salts and hormones. The first buds appear — the culture starts.

stage IV · 4–8 wks
Multiplication —
propagation

Every few weeks we divide the clump of shoots and transfer them to fresh medium. From a single explant — tens, then hundreds, then thousands of shoots.

stage V · 2–4 wks
Rooting
of shoots

Single shoots are moved onto a medium with auxin. Within 2–4 weeks they grow their own roots — almost a plant already.

stage VI · 3–6 wks
Acclimatisation
to nature

We take the little plant out of the glass. It goes into substrate, into a greenhouse with controlled humidity. Slowly — into real air, real sunlight.

§ 03

Why this way?

four arguments
No disease, no viruses

Sterile conditions = no pathogens. Crucial for aquarium plants — no snails, algae or hydra.

Genetic identity

Every plant is an exact copy of the mother. Varietal traits — colour, taste, habit — preserved without loss.

Old varieties live on

A hundred years of the Walim orchard recorded under glass. Trees that would otherwise vanish from the map — returning in their hundreds.

Scale without compromise

Thousands of plants from one leaf, in a predictable time. Rare species — available, without depleting their habitats.

§ 06

Frequently asked questions

FAQ
Q.
Is the plant in vitro a GMO?

No. Micropropagation is a vegetative copy — no interference with the genes. The same as taking cuttings, only sterile and on a laboratory scale.

Q.
How long does the whole process take?

From explant to a finished, acclimatised plant — 3 to 6 months, depending on the species. Multiplication is cyclical — each round yields more material.

Q.
Are in vitro aquarium plants safe?

Yes — that is their greatest advantage. No snails, algae, hydra or planaria. The plant goes into the aquarium with a purity guarantee, no quarantine.

Q.
How does the plant survive leaving the glass?

Slowly. Acclimatisation in a greenhouse with high humidity, gradually lowered. It is the most delicate stage — it takes care, but our success rate is over 95%.

Q.
Where do the old apple varieties come from?

From the orchard in Walim, where the trees have grown since the 19th century. We take a tiny fragment, clear it of viruses and start a culture. The old orchard — in new plants.

Q.
Do you work with nurseries and wholesalers?

Yes. We supply batches of aquarium plants to shops and wholesalers, as well as individual historic varieties for collectors and orchardists. Just drop us a line.
